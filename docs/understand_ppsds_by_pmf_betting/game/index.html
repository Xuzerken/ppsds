The math 

        

          The drift, as a matrix: the puck's next position is this times its current one. Every
          table here is linear, and the exporter asserts that before writing the game's data, so
          nothing on this page is guessing where the puck goes.
        

      

      
      
      

        The same game either way: the same draw, the same numbers, the same hand. The 3-D table has
        the depth on; the flat table is the same thing without it, and needs no GPU.
      

      
This run

      
      
      
      
      
      
      

      

        Each table ships a fixed set of runs of its own noise. A run is one of them: the puck's
        wobble is already recorded, so Run 3 plays the same draws every time — your stacks are the
        only thing that changes. The export records which run you played, which is what lets the
        script score it, so the list is fixed on purpose.
      

      

        What am I actually doing?
        

          Three things on this screen are the three things the package is made of, and the page is
          a second, visible implementation of the same rule.
        

        

          the moving pucka stochastic dynamical system with a known drift — the table you picked
          your stacksa probabilistic predictor: a pmf over where the puck will be, whose heights are its weights
          the payoutthe epsilon-logarithm score: log of the share of your bet that landed within ε
        

        

          The table's own movement is given — that is the setting: the nominal model is
          known, the noise is not. Only the percentages on the stacks matter: a weight on its own
          is a free parameter, which is why the page never asks you to pick one.

        Two averages of the same rounds, and they answer different questions. The payout is what a
        round gave back; the score is log of it, and the mean of those is a
        rate — raise e to it and you have the growth a round, which is what a bet
        rolled over every round would compound at. The average payout does not compound, which is
        why the two numbers differ.

How to play

    

      You are betting on where the puck lands next. Stack the felt; the dealer's ball decides the
      wobble; every stack within ε of the landing pays its share of your bet.
    


    
The gestures

    

      
Tap the feltput a stack there (it snaps to a grid)

      
Tap a stackselect it — the bar that appears holds the height slider, −/+, Move and Remove. Tap it again to let go. A tap on a square another stack owns selects that stack, so a tap never drops one on top of another

      
Drag a stackmove it

      
Drag the feltturn the table

      
Scroll / pinchzoom

      
Long-press a stacktake it away (desktop: right-click, or Delete once selected)

      
Ctrl/Cmd+Zundo the last change — a drag is one change, not one per frame

      
Ddeal / next round  ·  P hide the panel  ·  ? this guide

      
Any ? badgea control with a ? in its corner explains itself when you tap that — it is the text this guide would otherwise have to repeat. The switches are grouped by what they do: see, place, auto

    


    
The three things you will do most

    

      Put a stack down where you think the puck lands — its ε square is
      where that share pays. Set its height: tap it and drag the slider. The tower grows and
      every percentage on the table updates, because a share is a height over the total — and the
      percentages are the whole of what you are choosing, since a weight's own size means nothing.
      Deal, bottom right of the table: the ball lands, and the round tells you what it paid.
    


    
Playing it safe, and what par is

    

      Cover the table lays equal stacks so their ε squares tile the whole
      support: no landing can pay nothing, and the badge says what that guarantees. Where the noise
      is not uniform, that hand is safe but no longer the best — aiming at the fat part pays more,
      and risks a round that pays nothing at all. Par is the best average any hand could
      reach here: a line to watch, not a target you can beat. One table keeps its landing law to
      itself, so it has no par and no safety badge — read its landings and work it out.
    


    
How a run ends

    

      A run is the number of rounds you chose. It ends when they run out, or the moment a round pays
      nothing — that scores −∞. Either way the page stops, totals the run, and auto-play
      stops with it.
