        What am I actually doing?
        

          Three things on this screen are the three things the package is made of, and the page is
          a second, visible implementation of the same rule.
        

        

          the moving pucka stochastic dynamical system with a known drift — the table you picked
          your stacksa probabilistic predictor: a pmf over where the puck will be, whose heights are its weights
          the payoutthe epsilon-logarithm score: log of the share of your bet that landed within ε
        

        

          The table's own movement is given — that is the setting: the nominal model is
          known, the noise is not. Only the percentages on the stacks matter: a weight on its own
          is a free parameter, which is why the page never asks you to pick one.

How to play

    
Bet on where the puck lands next. A taller stack is a bigger share; every stack
      within ε of the landing pays.


    
The gestures

    

      
Tap the feltput a stack there; while one is selected, the first tap elsewhere only closes its toolbar

      
Tap a stackselect it; tap again to let go. Shift-click adds it to a group

      
Drag a stackmove it; with several selected the group moves, or nothing does if a destination is taken

      
Drag the feltturn the table; in Select mode this draws a box instead

      
Scroll / pinchzoom

      
Selecttap stacks or drag a box; the slider, −/+ and Remove then act on all of them. Turn it off to place new stacks

      
Ddeal / next round  ·  P hide the panel  ·  ? this guide

      
Any ? badgeevery non-obvious control explains itself. The switches on the table are see and auto; the rest are in the panel

    


    
The three things you will do most

    
Place a stack where the puck will land; tap it and drag the slider (or
      −/+) to set its share; press Deal and read what it paid. Height stops at 8× a
      fresh stack — a readability cap, not a claim about weights; the shares are what the score uses.
      −/+ moves a stack by 12% a press, or hold to repeat.


    
Safe, and what par is

    
Cover the noise support (Game settings → Preset predictors) lays equal
      stacks whose ε squares tile the support: no landing pays nothing. On
      skewed noise safe is not best — aiming at the fat band pays more and can pay nothing.
      Par is the best average any hand could reach: a line to watch, not a target. One table
      keeps its law, so it has no par and no safety badge.


    
Noise support

    
The prediction switch draws the law around the prediction in gold, at a
      low opacity: an exact box, weighted bands, or a 3σ window (a window, not a guarantee). A
      table that keeps its law draws nothing. The boundary is drawn over the chips, so you can
      always see where the law reaches.


    
How a run ends

    
A run is the number of rounds you chose; it ends when they run out or a round
      pays nothing (−∞). A settled round stops so you can read it. auto next round
      advances after a pause; auto all rounds also places and deals. Both stop with the run.


    
Teaching mode

    
The tour starts on every visit as a card in the table's lower-left corner, and
      a bouncing arrow points at the one thing each step is about. The page stays live underneath.
      The card's Next only unlocks after you have done what the step asks; Back
      returns to the previous step and undoes nothing on the table. Skip closes it for this
      visit and keeps the empty-table hint out of the way; the teaching switch next to Hide panel
      brings it back, and Show the tour below restarts it. Nothing is saved between visits.
